# Solo Play and Very Small Parties (PF1e house guideline)
Our table's guideline for encounter building with one or two PCs. It adjusts the official procedure in `library/pf1e/rules/encounter-building.md` (APL, difficulty, XP budget).

This is **not official**. It's the table's guideline, based on common community advice and our own test play. The official APL adjustment assumes about three PCs sharing the enemies' actions. A lone character faces all of them, with no one to heal, flank, or pull them out of a save-or-lose effect.

## Effective APL
| Party | Effective APL |
|---|---|
| 1 PC alone, gestalt or not | APL − 2 |
| 1 PC with a companion NPC, gestalt or not | APL − 1 |
| 2 PCs | APL − 1 (official) |
| 3 PCs | APL − 1 (official) |

**What changes the scaling is the action economy, not raw power.** The enemies' actions are split across the party's bodies, so a companion (a second set of actions, a second target, someone to heal or drag you out) moves the effective APL up by one. A **gestalt** character is more versatile and durable (better saves, more HP, more options), but still has one body and one set of actions. So gestalt doesn't change the effective APL. What it does is make the solo checks below less severe: a save-or-lose effect is less likely to land, and the one-crit test has more HP behind it.

Session zero can shift the scaling for the whole campaign, e.g. to APL − 3 or − 4 for a very forgiving game.

**Below 1, use fractional CRs** along the CR ladder:

| Effective APL | 1 | 0 | −1 | −2 | −3 | −4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Treated as CR | 1 | 1/2 | 1/3 | 1/4 | 1/6 | 1/8 |

Then use the difficulty table and the XP budget from `library/pf1e/rules/encounter-building.md` as usual, stepping along the same ladder. For example, a solo level 1 PC has an effective APL of −1 (CR 1/3), so an easy fight is CR 1/4 and a challenging one is CR 1/2.

## Solo checks: do these for every encounter, on top of the budget
1. **Class NPCs with PC gear count +1 CR.** NPCs with PC-class levels and good equipment (masterwork weapons, armor, a belt full of potions) fight like a PC of their level. The NPC Codex **Traitorous Brigand** (CR 2) is a full 3rd-level fighter with bull's strength and healing potions. Treat it as CR 3 against a solo PC. For a solo level 3 PC (effective APL 1), that makes it a **hard** fight, not a challenging one.
2. **The one-crit test.** Take the enemy's best attack, maximum damage, and critical multiplier. If one hit can take the PC from full HP to dying, the fight is harder than its CR, e.g. a greataxe at 1d12+4 ×3 can deal 48 against 25 HP. Either lower the budget, or use weapons with smaller multipliers, or keep the mercy policy from session zero in mind before the fight starts.
3. **Count the attackers.** A solo PC shouldn't face more than **two** enemies attacking each round, unless the extra ones are true minions (CR at least 4 below the effective APL). Enemies who arrive in waves are better than everyone at once.
4. **Save-or-lose effects** (paralysis, sleep, hold person, dominate, confusion, blinding) end a solo fight, because no one can help. Avoid them against a solo PC, or treat them as +1 CR and make sure there's a way out, like a short duration, an ally nearby, or the enemy taking the PC captive instead of killing them.
5. **Uncertain allies.** If an NPC might fight on either side (e.g. a frightened henchman or a captive who could switch sides), the encounter has to be survivable in the **worst plausible case**. Budget as if they join the enemy, or make sure their switching sides is prevented or delayed.
6. **Exit ramps.** Every non-trivial solo fight needs at least one way to end without the PC's death: morale (surrender, fleeing), bargaining, terrain to escape through, reinforcements for the PC, or an enemy who prefers capture.
