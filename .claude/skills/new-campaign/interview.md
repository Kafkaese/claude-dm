# Session Zero Interview

These rounds are guidelines. Adapt the options to earlier answers, and mark the recommended option `(Recommended)`. Earlier answers should shape later recommendations. For example:
- a gritty, deadly campaign → roll for ability scores or use 15 point buy
- a solo player → 20 point buy plus companions

## Round 1: Frame
1. **Scope:**
   - One-shot (1–2 sessions)
   - Short adventure (a few sessions, about levels 1–4)
   - Episodic campaign (loosely connected adventures)
   - Epic campaign (one overarching story, level 1 to 12+)
2. **Setting:**
   - Golarion (Recommended)
   - Claude creates a new setting
   - Homebrew from your material
3. **Content:**
   - Original adventure written by Claude (Recommended)
   - Published module or Adventure Path. Claude needs the text: ask the user to provide it, or warn that running an AP from memory is unreliable.
4. **Players:**
   - Solo
   - 2–3
   - 4+
   - If the party is small: should Claude run companion NPCs to fill gaps? Other options are 20 point buy, gestalt, or Automatic Bonus Progression.

## Round 1b: Inspirations
Ask in plain chat as free text. Explain that this is the best way for the DM to learn their taste, and that a quick list is enough:
- **Works they love:** books, films, series, games (video or tabletop), anime or comics. They don't have to be fantasy. For each, ask what they like about it if it isn't obvious.
- **Works or tropes they dislike**, and why, e.g. "chosen one plots" or "grimdark for its own sake".
- **Must-have elements:** anything specific they want to see, e.g. a heist, a rival party, a dragon.

Then work out the takeaways yourself:
- recurring themes
- tone
- pacing
- kinds of conflict
- kinds of NPCs
- the level of moral complexity
- how much humor

Share a short read-back, e.g. "Sounds like you enjoy morally grey factions, dry humor and slow-burn mysteries (The Witcher, Discworld, Disco Elysium)", and ask whether it's accurate. Use these takeaways to pick the recommended options in later rounds and the region suggestions below. For example, gothic works point toward Ustalav and horror.

Don't copy plots, named characters or signature settings from the works. Borrow themes, tone and structure.

## Setting follow-up (right after Round 1b)
- **Golarion:** Suggest 3–4 starting regions that fit the answers so far. For example:
  - Varisia / Sandpoint: classic adventure
  - Ustalav: gothic horror
  - River Kingdoms / Stolen Lands: sandbox, kingdom building
  - The Shackles: pirates
  - Cheliax or Taldor: political intrigue
  - Absalom: urban
  - Mwangi Expanse: jungle exploration
  - Ask whether the default era (about 4707 AR) is fine.
  - **Published adventures:** explain briefly that the DM avoids spoilers for published Adventure Paths and modules. Then ask which ones they've **played or read**, confirming what's already listed in `library/general/player-profile.md`. Spoilers for those are fine. If they list any, ask:
    - **Connection:** none / easter eggs (familiar places and cameos) / connected (its consequences and NPCs matter) / sequel (build on its aftermath)
    - **Outcome:** did their table's ending differ from canon? If so, get a short summary. Their version becomes what happened.
    - A connected campaign or a sequel needs an era after that adventure, so adjust the era.
- **Claude-made:** Ask for seeds:
  - tone
  - how common magic is
  - technology level
  - anything that must be included or excluded
  - The Round 1b takeaways already cover inspirations.
- **Homebrew:** Ask for the material. See step 2 of SKILL.md.

## Round 2: Tone
1. **Theme** (multi-select; if several are picked, ask which one leads):
   - Heroic
   - Gritty / dark fantasy
   - Lighthearted / comedic
   - Horror
2. **Flavor** (multi-select):
   - Dungeon delving
   - Urban / intrigue
   - Wilderness / exploration
   - Planar / weird
   - Others through "Other", e.g. nautical, war, kingdom building
3. **World pressure:** how actively does the world push back?
   - Lively: regular events on the road and at night, and enemies react when you get close (Recommended)
   - Calm: the world moves mostly off-screen, and interruptions are rare
   - Relentless: constant pressure, you're hunted, little safe downtime

## Round 2b: Challenge & Consequences
Explain that these questions set how merciful the DM is, both with dice and in general.
1. **Lethality:**
   - Standard: death is possible but rare (Recommended)
   - Forgiving: defeat usually means capture, loss or a setback, not death
   - Deadly: death is a real, regular risk
   - Brutal: plan on losing characters
2. **DM mercy / fudging:**
   - Mercy at low levels only: while characters are fragile (about levels 1–3), the DM may quietly soften bad luck, e.g. a crit that would kill outright. After that, the dice fall where they may. (Recommended)
   - Never fudge: all rolls stand, and monsters play to win when that fits their nature
   - Soft touch: the DM fudges or adjusts on the fly to keep the story going
   - Transparent: no fudging, but the DM may offer an explicit bargain instead, e.g. "you survive, but lose something"
3. **Consequences of reckless choices:**
   - Realistic but warned: obviously dangerous actions get a warning or a check to recall knowledge first, then the consequences land in full (Recommended)
   - Harsh: the world doesn't warn you. Poking the dragon means fighting the dragon.
   - Lenient: bad calls lead to setbacks and complications, rarely disaster
4. **Encounter balance:**
   - Mostly balanced, with some fights meant to be avoided and clearly signposted (Recommended)
   - Always beatable: encounters are built for the party
   - Living world: threats don't scale to the party, and running, negotiating or sneaking are often the smart choices

## Round 3: Style
1. **Structure:**
   - Guided sandbox: open world with strong hooks (Recommended)
   - Full sandbox: players set the goals
   - Story-driven
   - Linear
2. **Pillar balance:**
   - Balanced (Recommended)
   - Combat-heavy
   - Roleplay / social-heavy
   - Exploration-heavy
3. **Intrigue & mystery:**
   - Some: occasional secrets and twists (Recommended)
   - Minimal
   - Central: investigations, schemes, unreliable allies
4. **Branching:** how much should the story branch into side plots? Recommend the option that matches the structure answer.
   - Balanced: about 3 active side plots, each tying back to the main story (Recommended for guided sandbox and story-driven)
   - Focused: about 2 active side plots; the story stays tight (Recommended for linear)
   - Expansive: about 5 active side plots; a wide, branching world (Recommended for full sandbox)
   Explain that it's a soft limit: the DM follows the player's lead, parks extra ideas as dormant seeds, and lets a side plot grow if the player clearly loves it.

## Round 4: Characters & Advancement
1. **Starting level:**
   - 1 (Recommended)
   - 3
   - 5
   - Other
2. **Ability scores:**
   - 15 point buy (standard)
   - 20 point buy (high fantasy; recommended for small parties)
   - 25 point buy (epic)
   - Roll 4d6, drop the lowest
3. **Allowed sources:**
   - Core Rulebook only
   - Core plus the main Paizo rulebooks (APG, UM, UC, ACG, OA, Unchained…)
   - All Paizo
   - Paizo plus named third-party sources
4. **Advancement:**
   - Medium XP track (Recommended)
   - Fast XP
   - Slow XP
   - Milestone

## Round 5: Mechanics & Table Procedure
1. **Variant rules** (multi-select, adapted to earlier answers):
   - Background Skills (Unchained)
   - Hero Points (APG)
   - Automatic Bonus Progression (Unchained)
   - Unchained classes (Rogue, Monk, Barbarian, Summoner)
   - Mention others through "Other": Elephant in the Room feat tax, Wounds & Vigor, Spheres of Power / Might, Path of War, Mythic, gestalt, fractional base bonuses
2. **Other house rules:** Ask as free text. Common ones:
   - max HP at 1st level, then fixed average
   - 2 traits plus 1 optional drawback
   - critical fumbles or not
   - encumbrance tracked or not
3. **Dice:**
   - You roll your own dice; Claude rolls for NPCs (Recommended)
   - Claude rolls everything
   - You roll everything, including NPCs
4. **Hidden rolls:**
   - Claude rolls secret checks (Perception, Sense Motive) and monster rolls in secret, the way a real GM screen works (Recommended)
   - Everything in the open
5. **Rules approach:** (fits in the same AskUserQuestion call, since the house rules question is asked as free text)
   - Rules as written, with quick rulings when needed (Recommended)
   - Strict RAW
   - Rule of cool

## Round 6: Safety Tools
Follow `library/general/table-rules/safety-tools.md`. Do this in plain chat, and keep it light and non-judgmental.
1. Briefly explain lines and veils.
2. Show the checklist compactly and ask the player to mark topics as Line, Veil or OK, and to add their own. Point out that the two defaults are lines already, that unmarked means OK with check-ins, and that they can change their answers any time.
3. Explain the in-chat signals (X, pause/OOC, rewind, fade, check) in a short table. Make clear they can be used any time, without explanation.
4. Ask whether they want proactive check-ins before intense scenes: yes, only near veils, or no.

## Round 7: Table Conventions & DM Style
1. **Conventions:** present the defaults from `library/general/table-rules/communication.md` as a short table: plain text = player, "double quotes" = in-character speech, [square brackets] = instructions to the system. Explain the difference in authority in one or two lines: the DM has the last word over the game, while the user has full control over the system. Ask whether they'd like different conventions, and mention that they can change them any time, e.g. single quotes, other brackets, or a codeword.
2. **DM voice:** use AskUserQuestion, with a `preview` for each option showing the example lines from the DM voice table in `communication.md` (asking for a roll, reacting to a clever plan, ending a beat):
   - Narrator: a storyteller's voice, with no comments on your rolls or choices (Recommended)
   - Invisible: like a novel or video game, with no DM persona at all
   - Table DM: a classic DM who addresses you and reacts lightly
   - Showman: enthusiastic, compliments bold play, banter and jokes
3. **DM style:** ask in plain chat as free text:
   - **Narration:** concise vs. rich description, and how much NPC dialogue Claude voices
- **Character concepts:** any ideas they already have, so the campaign can hook into them.

## Defaults (quick mode or "you decide")
Golarion (Varisia), original content, guided sandbox, balanced branching (3 side threads), lively world pressure, Narrator DM voice, balanced pillars, some intrigue, standard lethality, mercy at low levels only, realistic-but-warned consequences, mostly balanced encounters, level 1, 15 point buy (20 if solo or 2 players), Core plus main rulebooks, medium XP, 2 traits, max HP at 1st level, player rolls own dice, hidden secret rolls.
The table conventions are always introduced, even in quick mode (one short table).
Safety tools are never skipped, even in quick mode. At minimum, state the default lines, explain the in-chat signals in one short table, and ask "Anything you want off the table or off-screen?"
